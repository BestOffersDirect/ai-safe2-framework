       Execution-Time Authority Requirements for AI Agent Actions
              draft-sullivan-wimse-execution-authority-00

Abstract

   Current work on AI agent identity and authorization establishes which
   agent is acting and what authority it was granted.  It does not
   establish whether the workload exercising that authority is still in
   an approved state at the moment it acts, or how much authority a tree
   of delegated agents has consumed in total.  A compromised agent host
   inherits valid identity, valid credentials, and valid delegations,
   and every signature it produces verifies.

   This document states requirements for binding the authority to take a
   consequential action to the state of the executing workload at the
   time of use, for containing consumable authority across a delegation
   tree, and for recording the result so that it can be evaluated later.
   It defines no protocol and no wire format.  It is offered as input to
   work on agent identity, agent communication, and transparency
   records.

1.  Introduction

   The AI Identity Management System draft [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims]
   composes workload identity and OAuth mechanisms for AI agents.  It
   ties credential issuance and renewal to an assessment of workload
   posture and leaves the evidence format and the verifier to
   deployments.  Credentials are short-lived, but an agent can act many
   times within one credential lifetime, and the posture that justified
   issuance is not re-examined at each use.

   The architecture proposed for agent communications
   [I-D.manyfolks-agentproto-arch] observes in its security
   considerations that valid credentials do not establish that a

   platform is uncompromised.  The use cases and requirements draft
   [I-D.ietf-agentproto-usecases-requirements] requires that credential
   validity be verified at time of use and that revocation propagate
   through delegation chains, and it places accountability and auditing
   mechanisms out of scope.

   These documents leave the same interval open.  Between the moment an
   authority is granted and the moment it is exercised, four things can
   change: the software and configuration of the agent, the policy that
   governs it, the standing of anyone in its delegation lineage, and the
   amount of authority its siblings and descendants have already used.
   An attacker who controls the agent host after issuance does not need
   to forge anything.

   This document states requirements that close that interval.  They are
   written so that existing mechanisms can satisfy them: remote
   attestation [RFC9334], workload identity, OAuth token exchange
   [RFC8693], and signed transparency statements [RFC9943].  The
   requirements are derived from an open source implementation described
   in Section 9.

1.1.  Scope

   In scope: what a decision point needs to know, and to record, before
   it permits a consequential action by an agent.

   Out of scope: agent identifiers and credential formats, discovery,
   dialog management, policy languages, the behavior of AI models, and
   any payment protocol.  Appendix A uses a payment as an illustration
   only.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP
   14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   Consequential action
      An action whose effect persists outside the agent and that the
      deployment has classified as requiring a decision before
      execution.  Each consequential action has a reversibility class:
      reversible, reversible with cost, or irreversible.

   Grant
      A statement of authority issued to an agent by a principal or by
      another agent, in any format.



Sullivan                   Expires 27 May 2027                  [Page 3]

Internet-Draft          Execution-Time Authority           November 2026


   Authority tree
      A root grant together with every grant derived from it by
      delegation.

   Consumable authority
      Authority expressed as a quantity that use depletes, such as
      monetary value, request count, records exported, or compute.

   Decision point
      The component that permits or refuses a consequential action.

   Posture evidence
      Evidence about the software, configuration, and environment of the
      workload that will execute the action, in the sense of [RFC9334].

   Revocation epoch
      A monotonically increasing value associated with an authority
      tree.  Authority issued under an earlier epoch is not valid.

   Accountable authority
      A named person or organizational role that answers for an agent's
      consequential actions and can stop them.

3.  Problem Statement

   A decision to permit a consequential action can be checked at four
   levels.

   Syntactic
      Signatures, audiences, expiry, and nonces are valid.  Existing
      mechanisms cover this.

   Semantic
      The proposed action falls within a deterministic reading of the
      grant.  Existing mechanisms partly cover this.

   Runtime
      The agent, its tools, the decision point, and the credential
      holder are in an approved state now.

   Temporal
      The grant, every grant above it, the posture evidence, and the
      revocation state are all still valid at the instant of execution,
      and the authority tree has not already consumed what the action
      would consume.

   Identity and delegation work addresses the first two.  The last two
   are where a compromised but correctly credentialed agent succeeds.
   The requirements below address them.

4.  Requirements

4.1.  Binding at Decision Time

   ETA-1
      Before permitting a consequential action, the decision point MUST
      bind together a digest of the exact action, the grant under which
      it is requested, the revision of the policy applied, a reference
      to the posture evidence relied on, and the current revocation
      epoch of the authority tree.

   ETA-2
      The binding MUST be fixed no later than the decision.  A binding
      assembled afterward from logs does not satisfy this requirement.
      This matches the decision-time binding requirement in
      [I-D.wadkins-agentproto-action-determinability].

   ETA-3
      The decision point and any key used to exercise the authority MUST
      NOT be readable or invocable by the AI model whose output proposed
      the action.  This extends the rule in [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims] that
      the model does not hold agent or tool credentials.

4.2.  Posture at Time of Use

   ETA-4
      For actions in a class the deployment designates, the decision
      point MUST evaluate posture evidence whose age is within a bound
      the deployment declares for that class.  Evidence gathered at
      credential issuance satisfies this only if it is within the bound
      at the time of use.

   ETA-5
      Posture evidence that is absent or older than the bound MUST be
      recorded as not established, not as a negative result.  The
      decision point MAY refuse on that basis.  This preserves the
      distinction drawn in [I-D.konda-agentproto-evaluation-state].

   ETA-6
      The bound SHOULD tighten as the reversibility class moves toward
      irreversible.

4.3.  Delegation and Aggregate Containment

   ETA-7
      A grant derived by delegation MUST NOT exceed the grant it derives
      from in any dimension, including scope, duration, counterparties,
      and consumable quantity.

   ETA-8
      Consumption of consumable authority MUST be counted against the
      grant used and against every grant above it in the authority tree.
      A decision point MUST refuse an action that would cause any
      ancestor's limit to be exceeded.

   ETA-9
      Where more than one decision point serves an authority tree, they
      MUST share consumption state or partition the limit among
      themselves in advance.  Independent local counters do not satisfy
      ETA-8.

   ETA-8 addresses a failure that per-grant limits do not: many
   descendants each staying within a small limit while the tree as a
   whole exceeds what the root principal authorized.

4.4.  Revocation

   ETA-10
      Advancing the revocation epoch of a grant MUST invalidate every
      grant derived from it, without enumerating them.

   ETA-11
      The decision point MUST compare the epoch bound under ETA-1 with
      the current epoch at the time of execution, not only at the time
      of the decision, when the two are separated.

   ETA-12
      A deployment MUST declare the interval within which an epoch
      change reaches every decision point for the tree, and MUST treat a
      decision point that has not confirmed the current epoch within
      that interval as unable to permit consequential actions.  This
      document sets no numeric value.

4.5.  Accountable Authority and Capability Tier

   ETA-13
      An agent permitted to take irreversible actions MUST be
      associated, in a record the agent cannot modify, with an
      accountable authority that is able to advance the revocation epoch
      of the agent's grants without the cooperation of the agent or of
      any AI model provider.

   ETA-14
      Where obligations scale with an agent's capability tier, the tier
      MUST be taken from the registered record and not from a value the
      agent presents.  A missing tier MUST be treated as the most
      restrictive tier.

   ETA-15
      Confirmation by the accountable authority, when policy requires
      it, MUST be bound to the action digest of ETA-1 and obtained over
      a channel the agent does not control.  [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims] notes
      that confirmation during execution needs further design; this
      requirement states the property that design has to have.

4.6.  Outcomes and Records

   ETA-16
      A decision MUST resolve to one of: permit, deny, escalate to the
      accountable authority, or unresolved.  Unresolved applies when the
      action may have taken effect and its outcome is not known.  An
      unresolved action MUST NOT be resubmitted until it is reconciled.

   ETA-17
      Every decision, including a denial, MUST produce a signed record
      containing the binding of ETA-1, the outcome, and for each input
      whether it was verified by the decision point, asserted by another
      party, or not established.

   ETA-18
      The record MUST be verifiable without the cooperation of the
      agent, the original session, or credentials that were valid at the
      time, consistent with
      [I-D.wadkins-agentproto-action-determinability].

   ETA-19
      The record SHOULD be expressible as a Signed Statement [RFC9943]
      so that it can be registered with a transparency service.  This
      document does not define a new record format;
      [I-D.nassar-agentproto-trail] and the profiles under discussion in
      the SCITT working group are candidates.

4.7.  Protocol Boundaries

   ETA-20
      When an action crosses from one protocol or administrative domain
      to another, the receiving side MUST NOT accept weaker assurance
      than the grant requires.  A grant that requires posture evidence
      is not satisfied by a bearer token presented on its behalf.

5.  Relationship to Other Work

   This document adds no mechanism to [I-D.ietf-wimse-aims].  It asks
   that the posture assessment performed at provisioning be available,
   with a freshness bound, at the point of use, and that authorization
   tokens be able to carry or reference the items in ETA-1.

   ETA-7 restates scope narrowing as required by
   [I-D.ietf-agentproto-usecases-requirements] for multi-hop delegation.
   ETA-8 and ETA-9 are additional.  ETA-16 is compatible with that
   draft's statement that an operation whose outcome cannot be
   determined stays unresolved.

   [I-D.nassar-agentproto-trail] defines signed action records,
   authorities that can stop an agent, and a rule that an edge passes
   traffic only while it holds current authority state.  ETA-12 is
   satisfied by that rule.  This document does not compete with it.

   Posture evidence can be conveyed using the architecture in [RFC9334].
   This document does not profile it.

6.  Security Considerations

   The requirements assume the decision point is more trustworthy than
   the agent.  If both run in one compromised environment, ETA-3 is not
   met and nothing here helps.  Deployments need to say where the
   decision point runs and why the agent cannot reach it.

   Posture evidence shows that measured software was running.  It does
   not show that the software is free of vulnerabilities, or that an AI
   model's output is correct or uninfluenced by injected content.  An
   agent in an approved state can still be instructed to take a harmful
   action that falls within its grant.  Aggregate containment limits the
   damage from that case; it does not prevent it.

   A counterparty that is authentic and malicious satisfies every
   requirement in this document.

   Shared consumption state under ETA-9 is a target.  An attacker who
   can reset or partition it defeats ETA-8.  An attacker who can delay
   epoch propagation extends the window in ETA-12; the fail-closed rule
   in ETA-12 bounds this at the cost of availability, which makes the
   propagation path a denial-of-service target.

   A decision point's signing key can forge records under ETA-17.
   Registering records with a transparency service limits what a later
   key compromise can rewrite; it does not help against a key that was
   compromised when the record was made.

   ETA-16 forbids resubmission of unresolved actions because retry after
   an ambiguous failure is a common way for an irreversible action to be
   executed twice.

7.  Privacy Considerations

   Records under ETA-17 link an action to an agent, a delegation
   lineage, and an accountable authority.  That linkage is the purpose
   of the record and is also a tracking risk in the sense of [RFC6973].

   Records SHOULD carry digests of action parameters and not the
   parameters.  Identifiers for the principal and the accountable
   authority SHOULD be pairwise or otherwise unlinkable across relying
   parties, with disclosure to an evaluator on a stated basis.  Posture
   evidence can fingerprint a deployment and SHOULD be referenced by the
   record and disclosed separately.

   Aggregate consumption state reveals the activity of an entire
   authority tree to whoever holds it.  Deployments SHOULD keep that
   state with the party that issued the root grant.

8.  IANA Considerations

   This document has no IANA actions.

9.  Implementation Status

   This section is to be removed before publication as an RFC.  It
   follows [RFC7942].

   The requirements were derived from the NEXUS reference implementation
   published by the Cyber Strategy Institute under the Apache 2.0
   license [NEXUS].  Version 0.6.0 implements the binding of ETA-1,
   registry-bound capability tiers (ETA-14), monotonic attenuation (ETA-
   7), aggregate containment (ETA-8), revocation epochs, the four
   outcomes of ETA-16, and hash-chained decision records with a redacted
   external form.  Each has tests that exercise the refusal path.



Sullivan                   Expires 27 May 2027                  [Page 9]

Internet-Draft          Execution-Time Authority           November 2026


   Limits of that implementation: posture verification is an interface
   with a test verifier, not an integration with a hardware root of
   trust, and stale evidence is refused without being recorded
   separately as not established (ETA-5).  The revocation epoch is kept
   per principal, which is coarser than the per-grant epoch of ETA-10.
   Consumption state is a single-host reference store, so ETA-9 is not
   met across hosts.  Records are not yet emitted as SCITT Signed
   Statements.  The implementation has not been independently evaluated
   and has no production deployment evidence.  Its bindings to external
   payment protocols refuse by default and have not been tested against
   live counterparties.

Appendix A.  Worked Example: An Irreversible Action

   This appendix is illustrative.  A payment is used because it is a
   familiar irreversible action.  The same sequence applies to any
   action in the irreversible class.

   A principal authorizes an agent to spend up to 500 units with a set
   of merchants over one week.  The agent delegates 100 units to each of
   three sub-agents for separate tasks.

   1.  A sub-agent proposes a payment of 80 units.  The decision point
       computes the action digest and collects the sub-agent's grant,
       the policy revision, a reference to posture evidence for the sub-
       agent's workload, and the tree's revocation epoch (ETA-1).

   2.  The posture evidence is 40 seconds old.  The deployment's bound
       for irreversible actions is 60 seconds.  The input is established
       (ETA-4).

   3.  The sub-agent has consumed 30 of its 100.  The tree has consumed
       410 of 500.  Permitting 80 would take the tree to 490.  Both
       limits hold (ETA-8).

   4.  The decision point permits, signs the record, and releases the
       action to a credential holder the agent cannot reach (ETA-3, ETA-
       17).

   5.  A second sub-agent then proposes 20 units.  It is within its own
       100.  The tree would reach 510.  The decision point denies and
       records the denial (ETA-8, ETA-17).

   6.  The accountable authority learns that the first sub-agent's host
       was altered and advances the epoch of the root grant.  Every
       derived grant is invalid from that point without being listed
       (ETA-10, ETA-13).

   7.  A payment that was permitted before the epoch change but has not
       yet executed is checked again at execution and refused (ETA-11).

   8.  A payment whose submission timed out is recorded as unresolved
       and is not retried until its status is reconciled with the payee
       (ETA-16).

   Step 5 is the case that per-grant limits miss.  Steps 6 and 7 are the
   case that credential expiry alone handles too slowly.
